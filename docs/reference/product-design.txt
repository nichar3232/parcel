Onchain Equity Desk
Product design for Stocklana
Version 1  |  14 September 2026  |  Product and engineering specification
Product definition
Build an integrated prime brokerage and derivatives desk for tokenized equities on Solana. Users manage stock exposure, borrow against eligible holdings, lend eligible stock tokens, trade options, and create structured products from one portfolio workspace.
The product owns the customer workflow, trade terms, collateral accounting, and settlement experience. Financing can connect to existing lending venues. The long-term value is making these services work together with transparent costs and portfolio risk.
The first user problem
A tokenized-stock holder wants to protect a position or change its return profile without moving between disconnected tools. Today, finding a counterparty, understanding the payoff, funding the trade, and tracking the resulting exposure can be separate tasks. The first release joins these steps in one usable workflow.
Build decision
Deliver the full desk design, with a working Stocklana core: one equity reference, custom bounded option spreads, a funded counterparty, verifiable Solana escrow, and settlement. Show holdings and collateral allocations in the same portfolio. Financing integration is a separate delivery track; stock-token lending and portfolio margin follow after the core is proven.
This release is a devnet demonstrator. A simulated quote or market scenario must be labeled. A production borrowing adapter is included only after an actual compatible market and transaction path are verified. Planned modules must not appear to execute real trades.
Success means
A holder can construct a payoff, understand its cost, and complete a funded trade with another wallet.
The maximum payout is covered before a position becomes active, and the same collateral cannot fund another obligation.
The user can see the difference between equity exposure, derivative claims, debt, and funds available to withdraw.
Stocklana explicitly includes structured products and asks for a working end-to-end demo with a real user problem. Submissions close 18 September 2026 at 4 pm ET. [1]

Primary journey
A user connects a wallet with tokenized equity exposure. The portfolio displays holdings and identifies the underlying reference. The user chooses Protect, sets a protection range and expiry, and reviews a payoff chart. A counterparty quotes a premium. Acceptance funds the contract, and the hedge appears beside the stock position. At expiry, verified settlement data determines the cash payout.
Secondary journeys
A trader uses the same builder without holding stock. The interface calls this a standalone derivative. A liquidity provider enters the opposite side of the requested payoff and sees required collateral and worst-case loss before signing.
A financing user allocates eligible stock tokens to a lending venue. The portfolio records the resulting debt and encumbered assets. A later stock-lending user lends tokens themselves, earning fees from a borrower who must return the owed token quantity under the loan terms.
Asset and legal meaning
The initial derivative references an equity price and settles in USDC. It is a new synthetic contract, not a tokenized exchange-listed option and not a claim on an OCC-cleared position. Holding the derivative does not deliver the underlying stock. Stock-token ownership rights depend on the issuer and instrument terms. [2]
Positioning
Position the product as an integrated equity financing and derivatives desk. Differentiate through usable custom payoffs, explicit collateral allocations, and observable settlement. Do not claim to be the first options protocol or a regulated prime broker. Historical announcements and hackathon projects establish precedent, but do not establish current liquidity or adoption. Hard boundary for the first build
One reference equity, one settlement currency, one expiry convention, and two bounded templates. Use a test equity token for devnet holdings; show the mint and network. A live asset directory may map to NVDAx or another verified Solana token, but must never relabel a test token as a real issuer asset.
The first derivative settles against the underlying equity reference price. It therefore carries basis risk against the traded stock token. The portfolio labels this clearly; the hedge is not guaranteed to offset token depegging or issuer failure.
Features deliberately deferred
Unbounded short calls, leveraged option selling, cross-venue portfolio margin, American exercise, barrier monitoring, autocallables, basket correlation products, and transferable short obligations require additional infrastructure. Their exclusion from the first release does not change the full product vision.
3 Application design
Navigation and visual language
Use five primary destinations: Portfolio, Trade, Build, Finance, and Activity. Add a separate Maker workspace. Use a restrained trading interface with readable numbers, neutral backgrounds, and green or red reserved for gains, losses, and risk states. Always show network and data timestamp. Desktop is the primary layout; mobile supports review, signing, and position monitoring.
Portfolio
Display net equity, cash available, collateral locked, debt, and estimated derivative value. List holdings with quantity, reference price, token price when available, and allocation. Group derivatives by underlying and expiry. Show realized PnL separately from estimated mark-to-market. Unknown prices display as unavailable and make aggregate values explicitly incomplete.
Trade and Build
Trade opens validated templates. Build exposes underlying, bullish or bearish direction, strikes, size, and expiry. A chart shows derivative payout and net PnL separately; optionally overlay the stock position. Show maximum loss, maximum gain, break-even when applicable, premium, fees, and protection limits. Changes invalidate the old quote.
Quote review
Show maker, quote expiry countdown, exact premium, fee, collateral, settlement rule, and total debit. Use one explicit Accept and fund action. After signing, show Pending, Confirmed, or Failed with a transaction link. A chart estimate must never appear as a firm executable price.
Finance
Use separate tabs for Borrow cash and Lend stock tokens. Borrow displays venue, collateral, debt asset, current rate, limits, and liquidation exposure. Lend displays borrow demand, available inventory, utilization, fee terms, and withdrawal constraints. Hide execution controls for unsupported markets; use Preview for features not yet implemented.
Position details and Activity
Position details contain the signed terms, participants, escrow amount, settlement source, expiry, and claimable balances. Activity records quote acceptance, collateral movements, borrowing actions, settlements, claims, and failures. Explain failures in plain language while exposing technical details through an expandable transaction view.
Maker workspace
Show incoming RFQs, portfolio exposure, committed collateral, and available quote capacity. Allow manual pricing and quote cancellation. Demo mode identifies the maker as a team-controlled test wallet. No fabricated depth, volume, yield, or counterparties.
4 Payoff contracts and examples
Bounded spread primitives
A capped call spread pays the price rise above the lower strike, limited to the distance between the two strikes. A capped put spread pays the price decline below the upper strike, limited to the same strike distance. Multiply by the contracted share-equivalent quantity. Payout is zero outside the profitable region on the other side.
Each product has a nonnegative, finite maximum payout. Premium is paid once at entry. Buyer net PnL equals payout less premium and buyer fees. Seller net PnL equals premium received less payout and seller fees. The deposit securing the payout is collateral, not an additional expense.

Illustrative capped upside
A $100 to $120 call spread on one share-equivalent has a maximum payout of $20. At expiry prices of $90, $110, and $130, it pays $0, $10, and $20 respectively. With a $5 premium, buyer net PnL is −$5, +$5, and +$15 before fees. The seller deposits $20 before activation.
Template validation
Reject reversed or equal strikes, zero quantity, expired contracts, unsupported decimals, and any payout exceeding escrow. Future custom piecewise-linear payoffs require a deterministic proof of the maximum across every segment and endpoint. Arbitrary code or unrestricted user formulas are not accepted.
5 Quotes capital and counterparties
Who takes the other side
The platform coordinates trades. A maker or another user accepts the opposing risk and funds the maximum payout. There is no assumption of an existing liquidity pool, minimum TVL, guaranteed execution, or protocol-funded yield. Without a funded counterparty, the RFQ remains unfilled.
Quote formation
The demo supports manual maker quotes. A model may estimate an indicative value using spot, time, volatility, rates and dividends, but the maker chooses the executable premium. Equity event risk, skew, hedge costs and token basis may materially affect pricing. A theoretical value does not establish that a hedge can be acquired at that price.
Exact RFQ terms
Every signed quote commits to the network, program ID, schema version, product template, reference feed, strikes, quantity, expiry, settlement policy version, USDC mint, premium, fees, maker, authorized taker, nonce, and quote deadline. The hash of these terms identifies the accepted trade. Versioned rounding rules are part of the terms.
Atomic acceptance
For the first build, the maker creates a funded offer account. The buyer accepts it in a single transaction that verifies all terms, transfers premium and fees, and activates the contract. Quote creation reserves the entire maximum payout. Canceling or expiring an unfilled offer returns the maker reserve. The program prevents cancellation after acceptance.
Funded offers avoid displaying the same maker capital as available behind many executable quotes. Support full fills only. Partial fills, offchain signed offers, and shared quote capital are later optimizations, each requiring explicit reservation and race-condition handling.
Collateral arithmetic
For the $100 to $80 put spread, the maker locks $20 per share-equivalent. With a $4 premium, the buyer pays $4 plus fees and the maker receives the premium. At a $90 settlement price, $10 becomes claimable by the buyer and the remaining $10 by the maker. A $60 settlement allocates all $20 to the buyer.
Fees and capital efficiency
Default demo fees to zero and show this explicitly. A production fee can be an agreed percentage of premium with a maximum total debit disclosed before signing. No yield is generated from idle escrow in the initial design. Full collateralization limits capital efficiency but eliminates reliance on a later seller margin call for these bounded contracts.
6 Price data and settlement
Reference policy
Pin one supported equity feed, denomination, exponent handling, and price-provider verification path per market. Pyth is a candidate, not an assumed completed integration. Verify current feed availability, access requirements, Solana verification support, and settlement-history retrieval before enabling real value. Provider documentation changes must be checked during implementation. [3]
Expiry convention
Use European cash settlement at a scheduled regular-session observation time, initially 15 minutes before the scheduled US equity close. This is an observation-time contract, not an official exchange closing-price contract. Store the absolute UTC timestamp with a validated exchange calendar that accounts for holidays, early closes and daylight saving time.
Deterministic observation
Proposed policy: use the earliest verified valid observation at or after expiry within a 60-second window. Freshness and confidence thresholds are market configuration snapshotted at entry. The implementation must prove which observation is eligible; it cannot allow a caller to select a favorable sample from the window. If the provider cannot support this selection and proof, change the policy before market activation.
Demo settlement uses a clearly labeled test oracle and an immutable scheduled sample. A live settlement route is gated on verified historical data and deterministic selection. Current spot may inform the interface but must not replace the contracted expiry observation.
Missing or unreliable data
If the observation cannot be verified, transition to Awaiting data and keep collateral locked. Anyone may submit the required proof later. A keeper retry is idempotent. Do not substitute zero, yesterday’s close, a DEX spot price, or a new reference feed after the trade. A prolonged outage resolution process is a production blocker; an administrator must not choose a discretionary settlement price.
Corporate actions and market closures
For the demo, exclude expiries crossing known splits, distributions or other corporate actions. Production needs contract-specific adjustments and a binding extraordinary-event policy. Stock-token rebasing can differ from the derivative’s share-equivalent units, so balance changes must not silently change a contract payout. Issuer mechanics must be validated for each token. [2]
Finality and claims
A valid observation fixes settlement once. The program allocates buyer payout and maker remainder, then permits separate claims. A keeper executes transactions; time alone does not execute a smart contract. Repeated settlement or claim attempts cannot move funds twice. UI access failure must not prevent direct onchain claims.
7 Prime brokerage and financing
One portfolio with explicit allocations
The interface unifies the account view while the initial custody and risk domains remain separate. Wallet balances, derivative escrow, externally pledged stock tokens, debt, and future lent inventory each have distinct ownership and encumbrance records. Never count an asset in two available balances.
Example: a holder has 10 stock tokens, pledges 4 to a supported borrowing venue, and retains 6. A later covered-call feature may allocate at most the unencumbered eligible quantity. The put-spread hedge is separately funded in USDC and does not automatically increase borrowing capacity.
Borrowing against holdings
Prefer a verified lending adapter rather than building a new money market. Kamino documents deposit, borrow, repay, and withdraw integration surfaces. Exact reserve support, borrow liquidity, token compatibility and network availability must be verified; SDK availability alone is insufficient. [4]
Flow: choose collateral and amount; retrieve current venue parameters; display interest and liquidation sensitivity; simulate transactions; sign; confirm actual deposited collateral and debt. Reject additional borrowing when fresh risk data is unavailable. Repayment remains accessible where the external venue permits it. Lending liquidations follow venue rules, even when our frontend is paused.
Lending stock tokens
This is a separate product from cash borrowing. A supplier lends transferable stock tokens; a borrower pays a fee and owes return of the specified token entitlement. Short selling also requires executable spot liquidity and a reliable buyback route. Lending a wrapper token does not automatically lend the custodian’s underlying registered share.
Before release, define loan term, rate accrual, collateral, liquidation, token-return units after corporate actions, recall policy, lender withdrawal queue and bad-debt allocation. Supply may be withdrawable only when sufficient token inventory is available. Do not promise instant withdrawal of borrowed-out tokens.
Portfolio margin as a later capability
A hedge earns a margin offset only when the risk engine and enforcement system control all relevant legs, collateral and closeout. An external lender cannot be assumed to recognize our derivative. Start with gross reservations. Later offsets require stress testing for price gaps, volatility, basis, concentration, oracle failure and execution liquidity, plus agreed default and loss-allocation rules.

Recommended implementation
Use a TypeScript React web client and a Rust Solana program, with Anchor if supported by the chosen toolchain. Use a shared typed schema for trade terms and an independent payoff evaluator for verification. A small database indexes events and RFQ metadata; it never authorizes custody or settlement. Pin dependency versions after checking current compatibility.
Core records
MarketConfig stores approved mints, token programs, decimals, reference feed, expiry policy, size caps and risk settings. FundedOffer stores immutable terms, maker, taker, deadline, escrow and status. Position stores accepted terms, parties and settlement state. Claim records track each entitlement and whether it has been redeemed.
PortfolioAllocation maps owner, asset, amount, venue or escrow, and encumbrance. OracleObservation stores verified source, price, exponent, publish time, confidence and proof identifier. ActivityEvent stores transaction signature, slot, event type, affected position and finality status.
Program instructions
Create market; create funded offer; cancel unfilled offer; reclaim expired unfilled offer; accept offer; settle position; claim payout. Administrative changes affect future offers only and cannot rewrite active terms. PDA derivation, account ownership, signer authorization, mint and token-program checks are mandatory.
Service interface
Expose market discovery, RFQ creation and status, funded-offer lookup, payoff preview, portfolio, and activity endpoints. Read responses include network, data timestamp and slot where applicable. Transaction construction returns exact instructions for wallet review. Wallet keys stay with users; credentials for data services stay server-side.

Trade and wallet failures
If an offer expires during signing, acceptance fails without a partial fill. If the wallet rejects signing, leave the offer unchanged. If a transaction result is uncertain, reconcile by signature and account state before permitting retry. Repeated client requests use idempotency keys, and program nonces prevent duplicate execution.
Market and service failures
When live price data is stale, disable new risk-taking and label existing marks as stale. When a quote is unavailable, show No funded quote available. When an external loan venue fails, show its position as unavailable instead of zero. Cached prices cannot be used to manufacture borrowing capacity.
Collateral invariants
For every active derivative, escrow covers the maximum contracted payout in settlement-token units.
After settlement, buyer entitlement plus maker entitlement equals the reserved amount, with deterministic rounding.
A quote can be accepted once; a claim can be paid once; cancellation and acceptance cannot both succeed.
Locked collateral cannot be withdrawn, lent, used as a second reserve, or redirected by the frontend.
USDC denomination does not remove stablecoin depeg, freeze, smart-contract or provider risk.
Token handling
Use integer base units with checked arithmetic, explicit rounding and bounded input sizes. Initially allowlist a simple settlement mint. Unsupported transfer-fee, rebasing, confidential-transfer or transfer-hook behavior must fail validation rather than silently change accounting. Production stock integrations require separate compatibility tests for the issuer’s actual token implementation.
10 Validation and demo acceptance
Financial correctness
Use an independent payoff implementation to check both spread templates at zero, both strikes, just above and below each strike, and very large prices. Validate fractional quantities and decimal conversion. Property tests must show that payout is nonnegative, never exceeds reserved collateral, and allocates every settlement unit exactly once.
Adversarial transactions
Test wrong signer, substituted mint or oracle account, wrong token program, altered terms, reused nonce, insufficient funds, offer expiry during acceptance, cancellation races, overflow, repeated settlement, repeated claim and unauthorized withdrawals. A rejected transaction must leave balances and state unchanged.
Settlement and external dependencies
Exercise a correct observation, stale sample, excessive uncertainty, wrong feed, missing data, delayed keeper and repeated keeper calls. Demonstrate that a client cannot choose a favorable observation. Verify corporate-action exclusions and early-close scheduling. For a financing adapter, verify the full deposit-to-repay cycle or explicitly ship read-only discovery.
End to end demo
Connect a holder wallet with test stock exposure and test USDC. Show the network label.
Create a downside-protection RFQ and inspect its payoff, premium and maximum payout.
Switch to a second wallet, fund the offer, and return to the holder to accept.
Open the confirmed position and show the real escrow balance and transaction link.
Advance the disclosed test-oracle scenario, settle on devnet, and claim from both wallets.
Return to Portfolio to see the stock scenario, realized derivative result and released collateral.
Shipping gate
The core passes only when the flow uses two real wallets, real devnet transactions and program-enforced funds. A clickable interface alone is insufficient. At least three settlement scenarios must match the worked economics. Recovery from one failed acceptance and one delayed settlement must be demonstrated.
Evaluation metrics
For user tests, target a first completed trade within five minutes and accurate identification of maximum loss and protection limits by at least four of five testers. These are proposed targets, not measured results. Later track funded RFQ response rate, time to quote, fills, repeat use, net revenue, maker capital utilization and settlement reliability.

This is a proposed four-day sequence, not a claim of staffed capacity. Protect the settlement and collateral work if time is short. Cut optional integrations and advanced charts before cutting the core financial invariants.
First controlled production release
Enable real value only after external contract review, settlement proof validation, incident procedures, issuer compatibility, and appropriate legal review for the intended users and jurisdictions. Start with explicit position and aggregate notional caps, one verified market and limited counterparties. Mainnet limits must be chosen from actual capital and risk evidence, not copied from demo values.
Expansion sequence
First add executable financing through a supported venue and multiple funded makers. Then add covered token calls and cash-secured puts with exact delivery and corporate-action rules. Add early close and long-claim transfer only when pricing and eligibility support them. Introduce stock-token lending once return obligations, liquidity and liquidation are credible. Portfolio margin comes last, after shared control and stress validation.
Revenue and distribution
Potential revenue is a disclosed execution fee on filled derivatives and a separately disclosed financing service fee where permitted. Financing costs and maker spreads must be visible. Launch with tokenized-stock communities and professional liquidity providers; validate actual hedging demand through RFQs before assuming recurring volume. No TVL target is required for the bilateral demo.
AI scope
AI may translate an expressed goal into a draft supported structure and explain its trade-offs. A deterministic validator calculates the payoff and required collateral. The user reviews and signs exact terms. AI does not select settlement prices, promise returns, invent quotes or receive signing authority. This feature is optional for Stocklana.
12 Decisions dependencies and sources
Defaults to implement now
Use one equity reference, USDC settlement, bounded European spreads, full maker funding, full fills, immutable trade terms and separate claims. Use one portfolio view with gross collateral reservations. Display financing and stock lending according to their actual availability. Ship a disclosed devnet core before broadening the desk.
Decisions that gate real value
Select the exact issuer token, reference feed, mint addresses and supported network; verify their economics and compatibility.
Prove deterministic expiry observation selection and define a binding prolonged-outage and extraordinary-event process.
Confirm a real financing market, API access and liquidation behavior before enabling borrowing.
Secure funded counterparties and establish how they will price and hedge exposure.
Define target jurisdictions, customer eligibility, issuer restrictions and operating permissions with qualified counsel. Tokenization alone does not resolve these questions.
